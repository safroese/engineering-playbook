These principles guide how I approach engineering problems, system design, automation, and continuous learning.

They continue to evolve as I gain experience.

## Engineering Principles

### 1. Automate Repetitive Work

Technology should eliminate repetitive manual effort whenever practical.

Every repetitive task is an opportunity to build something better. The best automation is often the kind that quietly saves time every day without requiring ongoing attention.

---

### 2. Solve Root Causes

Quick fixes have their place.

Permanent solutions have greater value.

I strive to understand **why** a problem exists before deciding **how** to solve it. Solving the underlying cause creates more reliable systems and reduces future maintenance.

---

### 3. Build for the Next Person

Every script, stored procedure, workflow, and document should be understandable and maintainable.

**Future me counts as the next person.**

Clear names, thoughtful organization, and good documentation make every solution easier to support and improve.

---

### 4. Measure Before Optimizing

Good engineering decisions are driven by evidence.

Rather than assuming where a problem exists, I prefer to measure performance, analyze the data, and optimize based on facts instead of intuition.

---

### 5. Simplicity Scales

The simplest solution that reliably solves the problem is usually the best solution.

Simple systems are easier to understand, easier to troubleshoot, and easier to maintain. Complexity should only be introduced when it provides clear value.

---

### 6. Learn by Building

Real projects provide lessons that tutorials cannot.

I believe the most effective way to develop engineering skills is by solving meaningful problems, experimenting with new ideas, and continuously applying what I learn.

---

### 7. Document While Learning

Knowledge fades.

Good documentation compounds.

Capturing solutions, decisions, and lessons learned creates a reference that benefits both future projects and future learning.

---

### 8. Design Systems, Not Just Solutions

I don't simply build queries or automate individual tasks.

I design systems.

Understanding how information moves from source systems through processing, automation, and reporting is just as important as writing effective code. The greatest value often comes from improving the entire workflow rather than optimizing a single component.

---

## Continuous Improvement

These principles are not fixed rules.

They represent the engineering mindset I strive to bring to every project. As I gain new experience, solve new problems, and develop new skills, this document will continue to evolve alongside my career.
